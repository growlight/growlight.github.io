Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 8, 2026

The short version. CouplesBudget stores the budget information you type in — amounts, dates, merchants, notes, categories. We don’t connect to your bank, we don’t run ads, and we don’t use analytics or tracking SDKs of any kind. Your data is not sold or shared for advertising.

You can use the app without an account, and then nothing leaves your phone. If you do create one, your household’s data syncs to our database and everything you record becomes visible to the partner you share that household with — that is the point of the app. Most of this policy describes the second case; the section immediately below says which parts apply to you.

Two ways to use the app, and why it matters here

CouplesBudget works with or without an account, and the difference decides most of what follows.

Without an account

If you have not signed in, the app never creates an account and your data never leaves your device. There is no sign-in, nothing is sent to our database, and we do not receive — and cannot see — anything you record. Your budget lives in the app’s storage on your phone, and the only copy anywhere else is whatever your own iPhone backup holds, under your control and Apple’s terms rather than ours.

In this mode we are not processing your personal information at all, so the sections below about our database, cross-border transfer, sharing with our hosting provider, backups, and what a partner can see simply do not apply to you. Exporting still works: Settings → Export Data runs entirely on your device. There is no account to delete — deleting the app removes the data.

With an account

Signing in with Apple creates an account, and from that point your household’s data syncs to our database so it can reach your partner’s device and survive a lost phone. Everything else in this policy describes this case. If you sign in on a device that already has data, that existing data is moved into your new account at that point.

Who is responsible for your information

CouplesBudget is an independent app built and operated by Jeffrey Liu. For the purposes of GDPR, that makes us the data controller; under PIPEDA, the individual accountable for personal information under our control.

Questions, requests, or complaints: support.growlight@gmail.com.

What we collect

Account and identity (only if you create an account)

If you sign in with Apple, Apple sends us a unique account identifier, and — if you choose to share them — your name and email address. Without an account we hold none of this: the app attributes your records to an identifier it generates on the device itself, which is meaningless outside your phone and which we never see. If you use Apple’s Hide My Email feature, we only ever receive a private relay address, never your real one. We never receive your Apple ID password. This information is held in our authentication provider’s user record.

Household information

The name you give your household, its home currency, an icon, the join code used to invite a partner, and the display names and roles (owner or partner) of its members.

The financial records you enter

The merchant, note and source fields are free text. Whatever you type into them is stored as written, so please don’t put anything in there you wouldn’t want your household partner to read.

Purchases

If you buy the paid version, we store a record that the purchase happened: the identifier Apple assigns to the transaction, which product was bought, when, and whether it has since been refunded. We keep this so the app knows your household has access, and so a refund actually removes it.

Apple processes the payment, not us. We never receive or store your card number, and the transaction identifier is an Apple reference — it is not a payment credential and cannot be used to charge you.

Information about how the app runs

The app keeps a copy of your household’s data on your device so it works offline, along with timestamps recording when each record was last changed and last synced. These timestamps exist to merge changes between devices — they are not behavioural analytics.

What we do not collect

  • No bank, credit card or financial institution connections. Every transaction is one you typed in yourself.
  • No card numbers, account numbers or payment credentials — the app never asks for them.
  • No analytics, telemetry, crash-reporting or advertising SDKs. The only outside service the app communicates with is our own database, through its official client library — and neither that library nor the open-source packages it depends on collects anything about you.
  • No location data, contacts, photos, health data or device identifiers for tracking.
  • No cross-app or cross-site tracking, and no advertising profiles.

The financial information in CouplesBudget is ordinary personal data. It is not “special category” data under GDPR Article 9 (which covers health, biometrics, political opinions and similar), and we do not knowingly collect any such data.

What your partner can see

Sharing requires an account. Without one there is no household and no partner — your data is on your device and visible to nobody but you.

Once you join a household, CouplesBudget is a shared-transparency app by design: every transaction, income record, note, merchant and budget in that household is visible to the other member — including records you created. There is no private or hidden spending mode.

Your partner can also see that your household has the paid version and which member bought it. They cannot see any payment details, because we do not hold any.

The order you arrange your budget cards in is stored per member and is readable within the household, but only you can change your own — rearranging your list never rearranges theirs.

If you leave a household or are removed from it, you lose access to that household’s data going forward, but the records you created stay in the household’s history and remain visible to the remaining member — they are part of that household’s shared budget rather than your personal file, and removing them would leave gaps in someone else’s accounts.

If you delete your account, they go. That is the difference between the two, and it is worth knowing before you pick one. See “What deletion actually does” below.

Who we share it with

We do not sell your personal information, and we do not “share” it for cross-context behavioural advertising as those terms are defined by the California Consumer Privacy Act. We have never done so, including in the preceding twelve months.

We use two service providers:

  • Supabase — hosts the database and authentication service that store your household’s data. Supabase acts as our processor and runs on Amazon Web Services infrastructure.
  • Apple — provides Sign in with Apple, distributes the app, and processes payment for the paid version. Apple’s own handling of your Apple ID and of your payment is governed by Apple’s privacy policy, not this one.

We may also disclose information if we are legally required to — for example, in response to a valid court order — or where necessary to protect someone’s safety or our legal rights.

Where your data is stored, and cross-border transfer

This section applies only once you have an account. Without one, your data is on your device and nowhere else, and nothing below happens.

With an account, your data is stored on servers located in the United States, in Amazon Web Services’ Oregon region (us-west-2).

If you are in Canada, this means your personal information is processed outside Canada and, while it is there, is subject to the laws of the United States — including lawful access requests by U.S. courts and government agencies. We remain accountable for your information under PIPEDA wherever it is processed, and we require our processors to protect it under comparable standards.

If you are in the European Economic Area or the United Kingdom, transfers to the United States are made under the European Commission’s Standard Contractual Clauses, incorporated into our agreement with our hosting provider.

Your rights

Wherever you live, you can ask us to:

  • Access the personal information we hold about you, and know how it is used and who it has been disclosed to.
  • Correct anything inaccurate — most of it you can edit directly in the app.
  • Delete your account and the personal information associated with it. You can do this yourself in the app — Settings → Delete Account — without asking us. If you never created an account, there is nothing on our side to delete; removing the app removes the data.
  • Export a copy of the data you provided, in a structured, commonly used, machine-readable format. You can do this yourself at any time, without asking us — Settings → Export Data produces your household's transactions and income as CSV files.
  • Withdraw consent or object to processing, understanding that the app cannot function without storing the data it is built to store.
  • Complain to a regulator — the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada, your EU or UK supervisory authority, or your state Attorney General, as applicable. We would appreciate the chance to address it first.

We will not discriminate against you for exercising any of these rights.

How to make a request

Two of these you can do yourself, immediately, without asking us: Settings → Export Data for a copy of your data, and Settings → Delete Account to erase your account.

For anything else, email support.growlight@gmail.com from the address associated with your account, or tell us the display name and household so we can locate your records. We may need to ask a question or two to confirm you are who you say you are before we act — particularly for deletion, since it is irreversible.

We respond within 30 days. If you are a California resident, we respond within 45 days and may extend once by a further 45 days where a request is complex, telling you why. There is no charge for a reasonable request.

What deletion actually does

Deleting your account removes, in a single step that either wholly succeeds or wholly fails:

  • Your sign-in record, so the account no longer exists.
  • Your profile, including your display name.
  • Every transaction you recorded, and the tags attached to them.
  • Every income record you recorded.
  • Any per-person budgets belonging to you. Where one still has other members’ spending in it, it passes to the remaining member rather than being destroyed with their data inside it.
  • The order you had arranged your budget cards in.

What stays is your household’s shared material: shared budgets, tags, and the transactions and income recorded by the other member — that is their data, not yours. If you were the last member, the household goes too.

One other record stays: if you bought the paid version, the purchase record described above survives deletion. It is the evidence that a purchase happened, which we may need for refunds, disputes and accounting, and Apple can still tell us about a refund for it after the account is gone. It holds an Apple transaction reference and no payment details. Ask us and we will delete it too, where we are not required to keep it.

Your spending history is genuinely removed rather than anonymised, which means your household’s historical totals drop by whatever you had recorded. That is a deliberate choice: “deleted” should mean deleted.

Two honest caveats. Your partner’s phone keeps its own offline copy of the household, and transactions you deleted may linger there until their app next reconciles. And residual copies sit in our encrypted backups until they age out within the 7-day window described above.

For California residents

From users who create an account, the categories of personal information we have collected in the last twelve months are: identifiers (an account identifier, name and email address from Sign in with Apple), and commercial information (the spending and income records you enter). From users who do not create an account we collect nothing, because nothing reaches us. We collect them for the purposes listed above, from you and from Apple at sign-in. We disclose them to our hosting provider as a service provider. We have not sold or shared personal information, and we do not knowingly collect or sell the personal information of anyone under 16.

You may use an authorised agent to make a request on your behalf; we will ask for proof of their authority.

How we protect your information

  • All traffic between the app and our servers is encrypted with TLS, and stored data is encrypted at rest by our hosting provider.
  • Your session token is held in the iOS Keychain, not in ordinary app storage.
  • Every database table enforces row-level security, so a signed-in account can only reach the household it belongs to.
  • We sign you in through Apple, so we never handle or store a password.

No system is perfectly secure, and we can’t guarantee absolute security. If a breach occurs that creates a real risk of significant harm to you, we will notify you and the relevant regulator as required — within 72 hours of becoming aware, where GDPR applies, and as soon as feasible under PIPEDA.

Children

CouplesBudget is not directed at children and is not intended for anyone under 16. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you believe a child has given us information, contact us and we will delete it.

Changes to this policy

If we change how we handle personal information, we will update this page and change the date at the top. If a change is significant, we will give notice in the app before it takes effect. The history of every revision to this page is public in the repository that hosts it.
